### What's new

- **Dates up to ten years ahead.** A date typed in words, such as `in 3 days` or `june 12 6pm`, can now be at most ten years away. A later date is turned down with a message that says so.
- **Deadlines.** `by friday 6pm`, `before sat at noon` and `friday by 6pm` now read as the date and time they name, wherever you type a date and time. Before, they were not understood.
- **A day and a time written apart.** `fri (6pm)`, `fri. 6pm` and `friday from 6pm` are Friday at 6 PM, and `friday night (9pm)` is Friday at 9 PM. Before, they usually came back as today.
- **A weekday on its own day.** `wed 9am`, typed on a Wednesday after 9 AM, is next Wednesday. Before, it was refused as past.
- **Repeats.** A date that repeats, such as `every saturday 8pm` or `friday 8pm every week`, is refused, and the reply says why. Before, the first was not understood and the second made one event.
- **Moved events on the public calendar.** When one occurrence of a repeating event is moved to a later day, or made to last longer, the public calendar now shows only the dates the event will really be posted on. Before, it also showed the dates that occurrence passes, which are never posted, and an event with a set number of repeats could stop short of its last date.

### How to use it

1. Close RSVPs the evening before: `/create title:"Game night" when:"sat 6pm" rsvp-close:"by friday 6pm"`.
2. For a weekly event, type the first date and set the repeat: `/create title:"Game night" when:"sat 8pm" repeat:weekly`.
